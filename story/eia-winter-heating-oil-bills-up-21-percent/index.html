EIA: heating oil bills to rise 21% this winter while natural gas bills fall 9%

The federal energy agency's winter outlook splits sharply by fuel, so what a household pays depends on how the home is heated.

By The Quillmark staff · Nov 11, 2026

What will it cost to heat a home this winter?

The U.S. Energy Information Administration expects the average household that heats mainly with natural gas to spend $640 this winter, down 9% from last winter. Homes heated mainly with heating oil are expected to spend $2,115, up 21%.

What about electric and propane heat?

EIA forecasts $1,196 for homes heated mainly with electricity, up 4%, and $1,246 for propane, down 3%. Natural gas was the main heating fuel in 46% of U.S. homes in 2024, and electricity is the main fuel in more than 40%.

Does it vary by region?

Yes. Natural gas spending is forecast to fall 14% in the Northeast and 15% in the South, but rise 7% in the West, where EIA assumes a much colder winter after an unusually warm one last year. Electric heating costs are forecast up 9% in the West.

What does EIA assume about the weather?

The forecast assumes national temperatures similar to last winter, with a warmer Northeast and a colder West. EIA also publishes side cases for a winter 10% colder or 10% warmer than its base forecast, and a colder winter would raise bills.

What can homeowners do now?

Before the cold sets in, the cheapest steps are the familiar ones: replace furnace filters, have oil burners and heat pumps serviced, and seal drafts around doors and windows. Oil-heat households may want to compare fill-up prices or budget plans, since that fuel shows the biggest increase.

Sources

  • U.S. Energy Information Administration, Short-Term Energy Outlook: Winter Fuels Outlook (Oct. 6, 2026)

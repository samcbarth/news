Home improvement spending expected to stay flat through late 2027

Harvard's remodeling forecast projects 1.5% growth to about $527 billion, citing flat building-product sales and stalled remodeling permits.

By The Quillmark staff · Nov 11, 2026

What does the forecast say?

Annual spending on home improvements and repairs to owner-occupied homes is projected to reach about $527 billion through the third quarter of 2027, growth of just 1.5%, according to the Leading Indicator of Remodeling Activity released October 22 by Harvard's Joint Center for Housing Studies.

Why is growth so slow?

The center pointed to retail spending on building products that has been flat to negative, remodeling permits that have stagnated, declining housing starts and broader weakness in the housing market.

What does it mean for homeowners?

A slower remodeling market can mean shorter waits for contractors and more willingness to price jobs competitively, especially for smaller repair work. It does not mean materials are getting cheaper, so getting more than one quote is still the best protection on price.

When is the next update?

The next Leading Indicator of Remodeling Activity is scheduled for January 21, 2027.

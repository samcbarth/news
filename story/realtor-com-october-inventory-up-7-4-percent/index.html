Homes for sale rose 7.4% in October as contract signings fell 17%

Realtor.com's October report shows more listings sitting on the market and fewer buyers signing contracts as mortgage rates held above 7%.

By The Quillmark staff · Nov 11, 2026

How many homes were for sale in October?

Active listings reached 1,183,500 homes nationally in October 2026, up 7.4% from a year earlier, according to Realtor.com's monthly housing report published November 4. Inventory is now about 6.6% below typical pre-pandemic levels, the narrowest gap since the pandemic.

What happened to buyer demand?

Contract signings fell 17.0% from a year earlier, and the number of listings in pending status was down 10.5%. Realtor.com tied the slowdown to mortgage rates above 7%.

Are sellers listing fewer homes too?

Yes. New listings fell 7.5% from a year earlier to 357,000. Inventory still grew because homes already on the market stayed listed longer: the median home spent 63 days on the market, one day more than a year earlier.

Are prices coming down?

The national median list price was $415,000, down 2.2% from a year earlier. About 20.5% of listings had a price cut.

Which regions saw the biggest changes?

Inventory grew fastest in the Northeast (up 16.5%) and Midwest (up 13.8%), with the West up 9.0% and the South up 3.8%. The South had the longest typical time on market at 69 days. For buyers in Texas and Oklahoma, that means more choice and more room to negotiate than a year ago, though higher rates still raise the monthly payment.
